## Tagline

A Christmas shop that sells what the war left behind, and shows you the tricks it used to sell it.

## Inspiration

Holiday shopping is where online stores use the most pressure: countdowns that never end, "only 1 left", add-ons that are already ticked, wording that shames you for saying no. We wanted people to feel those tricks rather than read about them. A haunted shop fits, because a dark pattern is a quiet kind of haunting: something in the page that wants more from you than it says.

## What it does

Re:Claim opens as Reclaim & Co. Holiday Market, a real-feeling Christmas store with search, filters, live 3D products, a cart and a checkout. A small bat has got in. Catch it and the same shop turns, in the dark, into the shop after a fictional war. The layout stays exactly the same, but every object shows whom it belonged to.

- Each of the 24 products is a real-time 3D object drawn in code. After the Reveal one shared shader burns, rots, dusts and tears them in place.
- Taking an object (Add to cart) makes it answer back inside its own picture. The watch runs backward, the doll turns her head to you, the candle's smoke shows a face, the shoes walk to the cart.
- Checkout asks you to pay with time, age or a memory. After Accept comes one earned jump scare, and then a confession written in blood: "Forgive me, Lord, for I have sinned."
- The Christmas shop returns, and a receipt lists every dark pattern the shop used on you and where it used it.
- An anonymous Echo means the next visitor finds what you left behind.

**Who it is for:** online shoppers, students and designers learning about deceptive design. It is also for anyone who wants a short, memorable story about taking things that were never just things.

## How we built it

- **Frontend:**
  - React 19 and TypeScript on Vite 8.
  - three.js with React Three Fiber and drei for 3D, zustand for state, GSAP for timing.
  - There are no image files at all. Every model, texture, "photo", backdrop and effect is generated by code at runtime in one of four ways: DOM and CSS, inline SVG, Canvas 2D or WebGL2.
  - One shared WebGL canvas draws all shop objects into their card boxes. A second canvas lives inside the product view's native `<dialog>`.
- **Story:**
  - A pure reducer holds the story, so it can be property-tested with fast-check.
  - A Director runs the timed sequences and keeps one anomaly on screen at a time.
- **Backend:**
  - The Echo service runs on Fastify with Node's built-in `node:sqlite`.
  - Values come from fixed allowlists, writes are rate limited, and it stores no IPs, user agents or identifiers.
- **Delivery:**
  - The app runs in Docker Compose (nginx and the API) on AWS EC2.
  - GitHub Actions tests every push and deploys through OIDC and AWS Systems Manager, so no keys are stored and no SSH port is open.

Details: [TECH_STACK.md](TECH_STACK.md) and [ARCHITECTURE.md](ARCHITECTURE.md).

## How we used Kiro

The full log is in [KIRO_USAGE.md](KIRO_USAGE.md). In short:

- **Specs:**
  - `.kiro/specs/reclaim` holds the requirements (EARS-style acceptance criteria), the design and the tasks, written before any code.
  - The spec was revised three times as the concept changed. The hiding ghost became the Day bat, and photos became live 3D objects.
- **Steering:** three always-on files keep every change inside the hackathon rules, the design direction and the code conventions.
  - Examples: one layout for both states, an effect only with a reason, reduced-motion alternatives, no unrequested changes.
- **Hooks:** they run the property tests when core files are saved, ask for a licence entry when an asset is added, and prompt this usage log.
- **Property-based testing:**
  - A coverage test showed that only 55 of 1000 random runs ever reached the haunted state, so the generator was rewritten.
  - A `-0` rotation broke the save/load round trip, and the reducer now normalises it.
  - Fastify was silently stripping unknown fields, and they are now rejected.
- **Agent and sub-agents:**
  - The IDE agent built and reviewed the work in rounds from Tanvir's feedback and reference images. It checked its own work with Playwright screenshots and frame-by-frame measurements.
  - Sub-agents built several products in parallel from a shared written brief.

## Challenges

- **Scroll lag:** a fixed WebGL canvas lags behind the page during scrolling. We replaced it with a canvas strip that scrolls with the document.
- **The `<dialog>` top layer:** a native `<dialog>` sits above every z-index. Effects inside the product view needed a portal, and the inspection needed its own WebGL context.
- **Haunted but readable:** the effects had to stay usable. Both palettes pass WCAG AA, effects flash no more than three times per second, every effect has a reduced-motion version, and every 3D object has a keyboard-reachable DOM control.

## Accomplishments

- **One shop, two states:** every haunted object can be traced back to its Christmas self.
- **Code-only visuals:** 24 products and the whole haunted world with no image files.
- **Tests:** 62 web unit and property tests, 7 API tests, and end-to-end tests on desktop, phone and reduced motion.

## What we learned

- A passing property test proves little until you measure what the generator actually reaches.
- Screenshots reviewed by the agent caught problems that code review could not.

## Testing instructions for judges

1. Open the live app on a desktop browser with WebGL2 (any current Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari). It also works on a phone.
2. Browse the Christmas shop. Open a product to turn its 3D object.
3. Find the bat hanging from the decorations. Click it, press Enter on it, or tap it twice.
4. In the haunted shop, add a few products to the cart, on their cards or in the product view, and watch each one answer back.
5. Open the cart, go to checkout, choose a payment, tick the agreement and Accept.
6. After the ending, open the receipt to see the dark patterns the shop used.

No account or login is needed. To start again, clear the site's storage (the story is saved in `localStorage`). The content includes a fictional war, blood, mild psychological horror and brief darkness. Nothing flashes more than three times per second, and reduced motion is supported.
